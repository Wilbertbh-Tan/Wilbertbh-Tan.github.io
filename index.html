About

I believe the most interesting problems live at the boundaries between disciplines.

My journey began in electrical engineering, where I developed a foundation in signal processing and systems thinking. This led me to data science, where I spent three years applying machine learning to sustainability challenges, from energy optimization to environmental monitoring.

After a sabbatical year building projects that excited me, I'm now drawn to the frontier of brain-computer interfaces and biomedical engineering. I see this as a natural evolution: the same skills that help us understand power grids can help us decode neural signals.

I'm currently seeking PhD positions and industry roles where I can contribute to research at the intersection of data science and human biology.
